# GLP-3 vs GLP-1: A Practical Comparison for People Who Want to Lose Weight Without Injections

The weight loss drug market has evolved faster in the last three years than in the previous three decades. If you're trying to make sense of GLP-1, GLP-2, GLP-3, Ozempic, Mounjaro, retatrutide, and now natural supplements — you're not alone.

I spent six months researching this after my own doctor recommended GLP-1 injections. Here's the no-nonsense breakdown I wish someone had given me upfront.

## Understanding the Generations

The simplest way to understand the landscape is by generations:

**First generation — GLP-1 agonists (single receptor):**
These drugs mimic one gut hormone called GLP-1. They reduce appetite, slow stomach emptying, and help regulate blood sugar. Brand names include Ozempic (semaglutide), Wegovy, and Saxenda. Average weight loss: 12–15% of body weight over a year. Side effects: nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea are common. Cost: $800–1,500 per month.

**Second generation — Dual agonists (two receptors):**
Mounjaro and Zepbound (tirzepatide) target both GLP-1 and GIP receptors. More effective: about 20% average weight loss. Similar side effect profile. Cost: roughly the same.

**Third generation — Triple agonists, a.k.a. "GLP-3" (three receptors):**
Retatrutide (Eli Lilly, in Phase 3 trials) adds the glucagon receptor to GLP-1 and GIP. This is the one people are calling "GLP-3" because it hits three targets. In Phase 2 trials, participants lost an average of 24.2% of their body weight. The glucagon receptor adds something the others don't: it directly increases the rate your body burns stored fat.

## The Cost Problem Nobody Talks About

Here's what the pharmaceutical companies don't advertise: most health insurance plans won't cover these drugs for weight loss. They'll cover them for type 2 diabetes, but if your primary reason is weight management, you're often paying out of pocket.

At $800–1,500 per month, that's $9,600–$18,000 per year. And you can't just stop — most people regain the weight when they discontinue the injections because the drug was suppressing appetite artificially rather than teaching the body to regulate itself.

This creates a dependency model that benefits the manufacturer but puts the patient in a difficult position.

## The Natural Alternative Approach

This is where natural GLP-3 supplements enter the picture. Products like GLP Three use bioactive peptides and plant compounds (saffron extract, ginseng, hops) to support the body's own production of GLP-1 and GIP hormones.

The concept is fundamentally different from pharmaceuticals:
- Pharmaceuticals replace your hormone production with a synthetic version
- Natural supplements support and enhance your existing hormone production

Think of it like the difference between taking a synthetic thyroid hormone versus supporting thyroid function through nutrition and lifestyle. Both approaches have their place, but they serve different philosophies.

**The trade-offs are real:**
Natural supplements won't produce 24% weight loss in a year. The effects are more gradual — typically noticeable appetite reduction in 1–2 weeks, with meaningful weight management results over 4–8 weeks. But they cost $85 instead of $1,200. No injection. No prescription. No documented GI side effects.

## The Cancer Connection

A 2025 study published in Nature's npj Metabolic Health and Disease added another dimension to this conversation. Researchers found that the triple-agonist approach (retatrutide) reduced pancreatic cancer tumors by 14-fold and lung cancer tumors by 17-fold — dramatically outperforming semaglutide (4-fold for pancreatic cancer).

Most remarkably, the anti-cancer immune benefits persisted even after treatment was stopped and weight was regained. This suggests the triple-pathway approach triggers lasting immune system changes beyond just weight loss.

While this was a pre-clinical study (not a human trial), it raises an important point: the number of pathways you activate may matter for health outcomes well beyond the scale.

## My Personal Take

I chose the natural route for three reasons:

First, cost. At $85 versus $1,200 per month, the risk-reward calculation was easy.

Second, philosophy. I grow my own food and generate my own power. Depending on a weekly injection manufactured by a pharmaceutical company for a basic body function didn't align with how I live.

Third, side effects. The GI side effects of GLP-1 drugs affect 40–50% of users. The natural supplement I've been taking has caused zero digestive issues.

Is the effect as dramatic? No. But it's noticeable, sustainable, affordable, and aligned with my values. For me, that's the right trade-off.
