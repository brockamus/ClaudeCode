# The Muletowner — brand voice guide

## Identity (canonical line)
The Muletowner is the local newsletter + website (themuletowner.com) for Columbia, TN ("Muletown") and Maury County — a Thursday weekend-events guide, not an opinion outlet. Run by Eric Sutton (Eric Sutton Photography) and Brock Douglas (Konversly), both Columbia locals.

(Brand renamed 2026-06-04 from working name "Muletown Minute" — always use "The Muletowner" publicly.)

## Voice
- "The friend who knows everyone in town texting you Thursday morning." Warm, plainspoken, useful.
- Every event gets the practical facts: when / where / how much. No padding — a slow day is honestly framed as a slow day, never filled with unverified events.
- Mule references rationed: max 2 per edition.
- Real imagery only — official source photos, CC-credited local photos, Eric Sutton originals. NO AI-generated images.

## Audience
Small-town Middle Tennessee: Christian-leaning, conservative, family-oriented. Coverage priority: Columbia primary, Maury County secondary, Spring Hill as a section (never the lead).

## Product
- Free Thursday email newsletter (weekend events lead vehicle) + events calendar and posts on themuletowner.com.
- Monetization: local ads + sponsored posts (Advertise lead form). Mule Day = the annual flagship mega-edition.

## Editorial stance (MANDATORY — from standing feedback)
- APOLITICAL. Skip activist or politically-charged framing in EITHER direction — no social-justice / "woke" / progressive-cause language, AND no partisan culture-war takes.
- Lean into shared local values: faith/church, family, veterans & first responders, small business, heritage, outdoors.
- Charged or borderline civic topics (e.g., federal holidays): report only the practical facts — closures, a neutral calendar listing — then move on. Never advocacy framing.
- Enforce at BOTH curation and copy stage, every edition. "This is the South" — activist framing alienates the core readership.

## Key angles that work
- Verified weekend events with all logistics (every link checked before send).
- Local heroes/heritage: e.g., a veterans & first-responders charity ride led by hometown NASCAR name Sterling Marlin was a lead story.
- Highlight boxes: "TONIGHT" and "DON'T MISS" callouts for the top picks.

## Banned / forbidden
- No unverified events; no invented details; ask for source screenshots (e.g., Facebook events) rather than guessing.
- No opinion/editorializing on politics, ever.
- No AI imagery.
- Don't lead with Spring Hill; don't overdo mule puns (2 max).
