# I'm a Homesteading Mom Who Found a Natural Alternative to Ozempic — Here's What I Learned

After my third baby, my doctor suggested GLP-1 injections for weight management. The price tag was $1,200 a month. The side effects included nausea, vomiting, and something called "Ozempic face." And the idea of injecting myself with a synthetic hormone every week felt like the opposite of the self-reliant life I'd built on our Texas homestead.

So I did what homesteaders do: I researched the problem and found a different path.

## The Problem With GLP-1 Injections

Over 40% of American adults are obese. The pharmaceutical industry's answer has been GLP-1 receptor agonists — drugs like Ozempic, Wegovy, and Mounjaro that suppress appetite by mimicking a gut hormone.

They work. But they come with real costs:

- **$800–$1,500/month** without insurance (and many insurers won't cover them for weight loss)
- **Weekly self-injections** with a needle
- **Significant GI side effects** — nausea in 40%+ of users, vomiting, diarrhea
- **Muscle wasting** — the documented "Ozempic face" phenomenon
- **Weight rebounds** when you stop — because the drug was doing the work, not your body

For someone who grows her own food, raises her own chickens, and generates her own power, handing my metabolism over to a pharmaceutical didn't sit right.

## What Is GLP-3?

This is where it gets interesting. While Ozempic targets one hormone receptor (GLP-1), a new generation of approaches targets three: GLP-1, GIP, and the glucagon receptor. People are calling this "GLP-3" because of the triple pathway.

The pharmaceutical version (retatrutide, by Eli Lilly) is still in clinical trials. But natural supplements that support all three pathways are available now.

The concept is simple: instead of injecting a synthetic hormone that *replaces* your body's natural process, you use natural compounds that *support* your body's own hormone production. Same pathways. Different philosophy.

## What a Recent Nature Study Found

A study published in *npj Metabolic Health and Disease* (Nature Publishing Group) found that the triple-agonist approach reduced pancreatic cancer tumors by **14-fold** and lung cancer tumors by **17-fold** — far outperforming the single-agonist approach used by Ozempic (4-fold reduction).

Even more remarkable: the anti-cancer benefits persisted after the treatment was stopped and weight was regained. The researchers found evidence of lasting immune system reprogramming.

This is pre-clinical research, not a human trial. But it suggests the triple-pathway approach does something fundamentally different — and potentially more beneficial — than targeting just one receptor.

## My Experience With Natural GLP-3

I've been writing about my experience with natural GLP-3 supplements on my homesteading blog. The short version:

- Noticeable appetite reduction within the first week
- Cravings for sugar and snacking dropped significantly

It's not a magic pill. It works best alongside the healthy lifestyle I was already living — growing real food, staying active on the homestead, managing stress. But as a natural tool to support what my body should be doing on its own? It fits.

## The Self-Reliance Angle

Here's what most health articles won't tell you: the choice between pharmaceutical and natural isn't just about efficacy. It's about philosophy.

Do you want to depend on a weekly injection that costs $1,200/month, requires a prescription, and causes your body to stop doing its own work? Or do you want to support your body's natural systems and maintain control?

For anyone who values self-sufficiency — whether you're a homesteader, a prepper, or just someone who doesn't want to be dependent on a pharmaceutical supply chain — the natural approach deserves serious consideration.
