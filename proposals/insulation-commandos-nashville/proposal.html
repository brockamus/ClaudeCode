Situation

You bought a business with a good name and almost no search footprint

Eight months in, the Nashville branch is running on referrals, reviews, and whatever the franchise site happens to rank for. That's a fine start and a bad ceiling — because the demand is already there, being searched for every day, and it's going to somebody else.

Here's the honest read from what I could verify publicly before we've had access to your accounts. Google has ten pages indexed under insulationcommandos.com/nashville/. That's the entire footprint for a metro of two million people. The pages that exist are decent — a noise reduction page, crawl space sealing, air sealing, cellulose, pest cleanup, testimonials, contact, incentives. What's missing is louder than what's there: no attic insulation page, no insulation removal page, no spray foam page, no duct cleaning page, and not a single page for Brentwood, Franklin, Hendersonville, Mt. Juliet, or Murfreesboro — the suburbs where the $8,000 jobs live.

The one place you do show up consistently is soundproofing. That is not a coincidence, and it's the whole strategy in this document. In Music City, the noise problem is the local problem. Nobody has claimed it.

Gaps

Four things costing you jobs right now

Gap 01
Domain

You're a tenant on someone else's domain

Your pages live in a subfolder of the franchisor's site. You don't control the template, the site speed, the internal linking, or what other franchisees publish — including the Clarksville location's Nashville page, which splits the signal Google uses to decide who ranks here.

Fix: treat /nashville/ as its own site. Build a complete internal hub, request the Clarksville-Nashville page be redirected or de-targeted, and stand up owned assets you control outright — Google Business Profile, a Nashville-only landing environment for paid traffic, and review velocity that no franchisee can dilute.

Gap 02
Coverage

Your biggest money services have no page at all

Attic insulation, insulation removal, blown-in, spray foam, and duct cleaning are the highest-volume searches in this trade. You perform every one of them. Google can't rank a service you haven't written a page for, so those searches go to Koala, iFoam and the roofers by default.

Fix: one page per service, written for Nashville housing stock — 1950s Green Hills ranches, East Nashville tall-skinnies, Williamson County new builds — not franchise boilerplate.

Gap 03
Geography

Nothing exists for the suburbs where the good jobs are

A Brentwood homeowner searching “crawl space encapsulation Brentwood TN” sees competitors with a page built for that exact search. You have a citywide page and a map pin on Trousdale Drive. Encapsulation work in this market runs roughly $7,000–$15,000 a project; losing those searches is not a rounding error.

Fix: six to eight suburb pages with real local specifics, each tied to the matching service page and to Google Business Profile posts targeting that area.

Gap 04
Spend

You may be paying for leads and can't prove what they cost

It looks like Local Services Ads are running, but nobody in the room could say what the monthly spend is, what a lead costs, or which of those leads closed. Meta isn't running at all — and Meta is where soundproofing demand gets created rather than captured: studio owners, short-term rental hosts, and parents of newborns don't search for acoustic insulation until someone shows them it exists.

Fix: audit and take over the LSA account in week one, stand up a tightly-targeted Google Search campaign, add Meta for soundproofing and STR-host demand generation, and put call tracking on every channel so we know what each lead actually costs.

The Wedge

Own soundproofing in Music City

Every insulation contractor in Nashville sells attic insulation. Almost none of them market soundproofing, and the ones who do are commercial acoustics companies quoting office buildings and studios, not homeowners. Meanwhile Nashville has the densest concentration of home studios in America, a short-term rental market with thin-walled units and noise complaints, and thirty thousand new residents a year moving into apartments and infill construction next to Broadway, Nissan Stadium, and I-440.

You already have a noise reduction page and you already rank on those terms. That's the beachhead. The plan turns one page into a cluster that owns the whole category, then uses the authority it builds to lift the core insulation pages alongside it.

Channels

How the three channels work together

SEO is the asset — it compounds and you keep it. Paid is the faucet — it produces leads while the asset is being built. Meta is the multiplier — it creates demand for a service most people don't know they can buy, and it makes every other channel cheaper by keeping you in front of people who already visited.

Search & Local SEO

Page build-out, technical fixes, Google Business Profile, reviews, local links. Everything above lives here. Slowest to start, and the only channel that's still working for you in year three.

No ad spend — included in management

Google Ads & LSA

Search campaigns on buying-intent terms, plus a proper handle on the Local Services Ads that appear to already be running. This is where month-one revenue comes from while pages age in.

Recommended spend: $2,000–$3,500 / mo

Meta

Soundproofing offers to studio owners, short-term rental hosts and new homeowners; attic and encapsulation offers seasonally; retargeting across everything. Cheap traffic in a category with no competition on this channel.

Recommended spend: $600–$1,200 / mo

Ad spend is paid directly to Google and Meta on your own cards — it never passes through me. Start at the low end; we scale on measured cost per booked job, not on a hunch.

Measure

What day ninety looks like

I'd rather be judged on specifics than adjectives. By the end of month three you should have all of the following, and you can hold me to them:

  • Roughly 18–22 new pages live on the Nashville hub, covering every service you sell and every suburb worth serving
  • A soundproofing cluster you own outright — the only residential contractor in Nashville marketing the category seriously
  • Every lead attributed to a channel, with a real cost per lead and cost per booked job for LSA, Google Ads, Meta and organic
  • Movement off page two on the core service terms — ninety days is enough to move rankings meaningfully in a local market, not enough to finish the job
  • A working lead engine you'd keep running whether or not you keep me

What ninety days is not: a finished SEO program. Local search compounds over six to twelve months. The three-month minimum exists because month one is mostly setup and the results start landing in months two and three — stopping at thirty days means paying for the setup and none of the return.
