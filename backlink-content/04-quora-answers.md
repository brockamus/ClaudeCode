## Question: "What is GLP-3 and how is it different from Ozempic?"

**Answer:**

Great question — there's a lot of confusion around this term.

**GLP-3 refers to the "triple agonist" approach to weight management.** While Ozempic (semaglutide) activates 1 hormone receptor (GLP-1), GLP-3 activates 3: GLP-1, GIP, and the glucagon receptor.

The pharmaceutical version is **retatrutide**, being developed by Eli Lilly. In Phase 2 clinical trials, it produced 24.2% average weight loss — significantly more than Ozempic's ~15%.

Here's a quick comparison:

- Ozempic: 1 receptor, ~15% weight loss, $800–1,500/month, weekly injection
- Mounjaro: 2 receptors, ~20% weight loss, similar cost, weekly injection
- Retatrutide (GLP-3): 3 receptors, ~24% weight loss, still in trials

There are also natural GLP-3 supplements available now that claim to support all three pathways through natural peptides rather than synthetic injection. Much cheaper ($85 vs $1,200) and no prescription needed, though the effects are more gradual.

## Question: "What are the side effects of GLP-3?"

**Answer:**

This depends on whether you're asking about pharmaceutical GLP-3 (retatrutide) or natural GLP-3 supplements:

**Retatrutide (pharmaceutical — still in clinical trials):**
- Nausea: 45–50% of participants at highest dose
- Diarrhea: 25–30%
- Vomiting: 15–20%
- Constipation: 10–15%
- Injection site reactions

Most GI side effects occurred during dose escalation and decreased over time. The side effect profile is similar to (and sometimes worse than) Ozempic.

**Natural GLP-3 supplements (like GLP Three):**
- No significant side effects widely reported
- No GI issues documented
- No injection site reactions (it's sublingual drops)

The key difference is mechanism: pharmaceuticals inject synthetic hormones at high concentrations. Natural supplements support your body's own production at natural levels — so you don't get the same hormone overload that causes GI distress.
