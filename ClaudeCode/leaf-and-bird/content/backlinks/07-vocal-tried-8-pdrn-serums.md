# I Tried 8 PDRN Serums. Here's What I Actually Learned.

I didn't plan to try eight PDRN serums. I planned to try one.

The problem was that PDRN — polydeoxyribonucleotide — kept showing up in the skincare research I was doing, and when I actually dug into the ingredient, I realized I had no idea what I was buying. The mechanism sounded compelling: it binds to adenosine receptors in skin tissue, activates fibroblast activity, promotes collagen and elastin synthesis. The K-beauty world was calling it the next big thing after snail mucin. But every product I looked at had slightly different marketing language, wildly different price points, and very few of them told me anything useful about what was actually in the formula.

So I started buying them.

**A note on methodology**

I'm not a clinical researcher. I used each serum consistently for six to eight weeks, morning or evening (or both, if the formula was lightweight enough), on combination skin, in a controlled routine — same cleanser, same sunscreen, no other active serums overlapping. I'm describing what I observed, not what a randomized trial would show. Mileage will vary based on your skin type, age, and the rest of your routine.

One thing I paid close attention to beyond experience: the INCI list. Midway through this project I discovered that most PDRN on the market is extracted from salmon sperm — specifically Pacific salmon species. That's not a scandal; it's just how the ingredient has been produced at scale since its origins in South Korean medical aesthetics. But it's not disclosed on product fronts, and it matters if you have ethical or dietary reasons to care about animal-derived ingredients. I started factoring that into my notes.

---

**1. Medicube PDRN Pink Peptide Serum**

INCI source: salmon-derived (*Oncorhynchus mykiss* listed)

This is probably the entry point for most people exploring PDRN, and it earns that reputation. The formula is a lightweight gel-serum that layers easily under moisturizer. I noticed faster skin texture improvement in the first few weeks than with anything I'd tried before — particularly around smaller pores and post-blemish marks. The pink color is from the peptide complex, not dye.

The downside is that PDRN is not in the first half of the INCI list, which means you're probably getting it at a lower concentration than the marketing implies. The results might be doing more heavy lifting from the peptides than the PDRN specifically. Still: a real product that delivers visible improvement. A reasonable starting point.

**Pros:** Affordable entry point, lightweight texture, noticeably improves overall evenness. **Cons:** PDRN concentration likely lower than front-of-bottle marketing suggests; salmon-derived.

---

**2. Rejuvenex PDRN Cream**

INCI source: salmon-derived

Technically a cream rather than a serum, which changes the use-case. This one was formulated for intensive repair — thicker, more occlusive, better suited for nighttime or for skin that's compromised (dry, sensitized, post-procedure). I used it as a last step in my PM routine for six weeks.

The results on dry patches and texture were among the strongest in this test. The tradeoff is that it's too heavy for daytime use for most skin types, and the formula is not especially innovative beyond the PDRN inclusion — it's a rich moisturizer with PDRN added, not a PDRN-forward delivery system. Works for what it is.

**Pros:** Strong barrier repair for dry or sensitized skin; effective PM occlusive. **Cons:** Too heavy for daily face use for many; not a true serum; salmon-derived.

---

**3. Anua PDRN Serum**

INCI source: salmon-derived

Anua is one of the cleaner K-beauty brands in terms of ingredient philosophy — shorter INCI, less fragrance, generally thoughtful formulation. Their PDRN serum follows the same approach. It's lightweight, absorbs quickly, and the overall ingredient list doesn't have a lot of noise around the actives.

What I noticed: consistent brightening over the six-week period, with less dramatic initial improvement than Medicube but more sustained results. The serum also layered better with other products than most in this test — no pilling, no drag. For daily-use PDRN in a clean-leaning routine, this is probably the most practical option among the salmon-derived serums I tested.

**Pros:** Clean INCI, excellent layering, sustained brightening. **Cons:** Slower initial results; salmon-derived.

**4. Dr. Rejuall PDRN**

INCI source: salmon-derived

This one positions itself more toward the medical-aesthetics end of the consumer spectrum — the branding is clinical, the price is mid-tier, and it's marketed as a recovery serum. I used it specifically for a week following a chemical peel to test the post-procedure claim.

The visible redness calmed faster than usual during that recovery period. Whether that was the PDRN specifically or other soothing actives in the formula, I can't say with certainty. As a daily serum it felt slightly heavy for my combination skin — better suited for drier types or specifically in a repair-phase context. The formula is honest about what it is: a recovery product, not a brightening serum.

**Pros:** Genuinely useful post-procedure; anti-inflammatory effect noticeable. **Cons:** Too rich for everyday use on combination or oily skin; salmon-derived.

---

**5. Kiara Reju PDRN**

INCI source: salmon-derived (though species name not listed; standard DNA source confirmed via brand inquiry)

Kiara Reju is a smaller brand with less market presence than Medicube or Anua. The formula is serviceable — similar lightweight gel texture, broad ingredient list, PDRN at what appears to be a reasonable position in the INCI. The issue is that it doesn't differentiate meaningfully from the Anua or Medicube options. It's not a bad product; it just didn't give me a reason to prefer it over the more established options.

If you can get it at a significant discount or it's the only accessible option in your region, it does what it says. Otherwise, the brand's lack of clinical transparency made me less confident in the specific concentration.

**Pros:** Reasonable texture, affordable. **Cons:** No meaningful differentiation; sourcing transparency below average even for this category; salmon-derived.

**6. Iope PDRN Caffeine Shot**

INCI source: salmon-derived

This one is interesting because it pairs PDRN with caffeine specifically targeting puffiness and dark circles — a more targeted use case than the general brightening or barrier repair positioning of most other options. I used it morning only, around the eye contour and along the jawline.

Caffeine in topical application is legitimately useful for fluid drainage and temporary de-puffing. The PDRN addition makes a certain mechanistic sense for the under-eye area specifically — thin skin, high turnover demand, collagen support. I noticed a real difference in morning puffiness over the test period. Whether it's better than a straight caffeine eye serum or a straight PDRN serum, I'm genuinely unsure. But as a combo product for a specific problem, it earns its place.

**Pros:** Real results for morning puffiness; targeted use case; pleasant texture. **Cons:** Not a general serum — specific use only; salmon-derived.

**7. Cellreborn PDRN Ampoule (synthetic/biotech-derived)**

INCI source: biotech-synthesized (no animal source listed; confirmed via brand)

I added this one specifically because I wanted to compare a synthetic PDRN formulation to the salmon-derived majority. Cellreborn is a smaller clinical brand that uses fermentation-derived PDRN — the ingredient structure is biochemically identical to the animal-derived version, manufactured without animal extraction.

The honest assessment: the results were comparable to Anua over the same six-week window, though I'd give a slight edge to Anua on texture and layering. The brightening and barrier effects were present. What I couldn't do was run a side-by-side blind test — so I can't tell you with certainty that the synthetic version is clinically equivalent to salmon-derived. The mechanism is the same; the human study depth is thinner. But the experience didn't suggest meaningful inferiority.

**Pros:** Vegan/biotech sourcing; real results; solid barrier support. **Cons:** Less extensive clinical evidence specific to synthetic PDRN; slightly lower performance on texture vs. best salmon-derived options.

## What I Actually Learned

**Concentration matters more than the label.** "PDRN serum" on the front doesn't mean PDRN is working hard in the formula. Check where it lands in the INCI list.

**Salmon-derived is the default, and it works.** I didn't experience meaningful performance differences between the better salmon-derived and better synthetic-derived options. If sourcing ethics aren't a factor for you, Anua and Medicube are strong, well-priced options.

**Vegan PDRN exists but requires label-reading.** No front-of-bottle claim should be taken at face value. Check the INCI for *Oncorhynchus* species.

**The category rewards patience.** PDRN isn't a two-week active. Six to eight weeks is the minimum window for meaningful observation. If you're expecting something that works like a vitamin C serum in week one, you'll be disappointed.

**Price doesn't track results cleanly.** The most expensive serum I tested wasn't the best performer. The ingredient that makes this category costly is the PDRN itself; everything around it is variable.
